    What the bytes prove
    
              
    A formula can generate 7,500 cells without storing 7,500 manually maintained answers as the authority. One corrected formula or rounding rule updates the generated result consistently. Static tables can still be useful outputs, but they are outputs, not the calculation authority. Images and presentation assets can be larger than the mathematical engine, so the report separates them. Storage size is only one benefit; auditability, reproducibility, drift reduction, and testability are the larger benefits.
    
            
    
            
    
              
    What the bytes do not prove
    
              
    Small files do not prove a formula is correct. The proof comes from visible equations, source notes, deterministic generation, official checkpoints, cell-by-cell audits, and tests that fail when the status model or rounding behavior drifts.
